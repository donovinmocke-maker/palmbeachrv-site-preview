What Most Boat Owners Figure Out Too Late

Fort Myers on a Saturday morning has its own rhythm. You want to be on the Caloosahatchee before the ramps back up and before the heat settles in. The boat makes that morning possible. The spot you keep it in determines how that morning actually starts. For a lot of Lee County boat owners, the friction begins before they ever reach the water, and most of it traces back to a decision they made, or didn’t make, about where the boat lives between trips.

The Boat Storage Problem That Most Lee County Boat Owners Hit Eventually

Lee County has 48,251 registered vessels on the water (FLHSMV, August 2025). It also has a growing number of HOA-governed neighborhoods. In Florida, those two things create a problem that most boat owners handle poorly until they can’t anymore.

The letter shows up. One paragraph. The vessel is visible from the street, in violation of the community’s storage provisions. Fourteen days to remedy or request a hearing. Most people put it on the kitchen counter and say they’ll handle it.

Florida Statute 720.3045 is why the driveway was never a real solution to begin with. It restricts storage visible from frontage, adjacent parcels, adjacent common areas, or a community golf course. In most planned communities, meeting that threshold is impossible regardless of how careful you are about timing. The side yard doesn’t work either. Moving the boat before a board walk-through doesn’t change the statute.

When enforcement starts, it follows a defined process under Florida Statute 720.305: notice, hearing, and fines up to $100 per day for a continuing violation, up to $1,000 per cycle. That clock runs whether the boat has been there a week or three years.

The common response is to wait. Find a spot after the letter arrives. The problem is that facilities in Lee County fill fast. Around 200 people were on the waitlist for Palm Beach RV and Boat Storage before it even opened. The enforcement notice and the full waitlist tend to arrive at the same time.

And when you do find a spot, where the boat actually lives matters as much as getting it off the driveway.

What a Few Seasons of Unprotected Boat Storage in Fort Myers Actually Costs

For a 20 to 25-foot center console stored outside and uncovered in Fort Myers, a few seasons of Florida sun runs up a repair bill between $6,612 and $13,356. That is the actual cost comparison when covered storage feels expensive.

Here is how it gets there.

Fort Myers UV Index regularly reaches 9 to 11, rated Very High to Extreme, during peak season. That exposure doesn’t just fade the finish. It breaks down the molecular structure of gelcoat, making it porous. Salt finds its way in. Oxidation accelerates. By the end of the first season the hull looks dull. By the second it’s chalky. Restoring it runs $900–$1,750 in Southwest Florida at published local rates. Left past the point of restoration, a full re-gelcoat is $6,000–$10,000.

The canvas goes next. The Bimini starts fading in the first year and cracking by the third. Replacing it in South Florida runs $800–$1,800. A full cover replacement adds another $1,400–$2,250.

Then the upholstery. Florida humidity gets into the foam. The vinyl dries out and splits. A full interior on an 18 to 25-foot boat runs $3,500–$8,000 at South Florida shops. Individual cushions are $245–$295 each before you factor in foam replacement.

None of that includes what it does to resale value. It is just the catch-up bill.

What to Look for in a Fort Myers Boat Storage Facility

How a facility is designed matters more than most owners realize until they’ve already lost a tail light backing in.

Real owner complaints from facilities across Florida tell the same story: gate clearances measured in inches, first-entry damage, maneuvers that took over an hour, doors that barely opened wide enough to get inside the spot. These aren’t rare. They’re what happens when a standard parking lot gets converted into storage for vehicles it was never built to hold.

A facility designed for boats and RVs means wide drive lanes, pull-through spots for owners who’d rather not back in, and 15-foot clearance height, which fits anything legal on the road. See available spot sizes at Palm Beach RV and Boat Storage.

The other piece most Fort Myers storage facilities skip is what happens after a trip on the Caloosahatchee or the Gulf. Mercury Marine and Yamaha both document what happens when a boat comes back from saltwater or brackish water without flushing the motor: corrosion in the water passages, compromised cooling, shortened engine life. An enclosed wash bay with a motor flush station at the storage facility is basic maintenance infrastructure. It keeps the engine working between trips. Every stop you don’t have to make on the way home is time back on the water.

The last piece is what happens to the boat while you’re not there at all.

What Secure Storage Actually Looks Like

Florida led the nation in watercraft thefts in 2021 with 791 incidents. Ninety percent of stolen boats are taken while on their trailers. Only around 15 percent of owners had any lock installed at the time of theft.

The standard answer from a gated lot is cameras and a keypad. Owners who have been through a theft at a gated, surveilled facility know how far that goes.

Concrete block perimeter walls are a different category from chain-link. A single entry point where every tenant has their own gate code is different from a shared keypad. Staff on site seven days a week during business hours is different from an automated system with a callback number. See how the facility is built.
